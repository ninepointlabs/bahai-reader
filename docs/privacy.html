PRIVACY

Your reading stays yours.

Bahá’í Reader is a free, offline reader of Bahá’í prayers and writings. There is no account to create, and the app has no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. This page explains, plainly, what the app keeps and the few times it uses the network.

Last updated 24 November 2026. This applies to the app on Linux, Android and Windows.

In short

  • No account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting.
  • Your highlights, notes, saved passages, reading position and settings are kept on your device. We have no server, and nothing is sent to us.
  • Books you import into Study are copied into the app on your device and are never sent anywhere.
  • Your notes leave your device only in the ways you choose: a file you export, a folder you choose to sync through, or, on Android, Android’s own backup, which you can turn off.
  • A location is optional. It is used on your device to work out sunrise and sunset, and is never sent anywhere.
  • The Android app has no network access at all. On Linux and Windows, the app goes online only when you ask it to download a language or check the list of languages.

What stays on your device

Everything you make in the app is saved in files on your device: highlights, notes, saved passages, where you were reading, the language you chose, and your settings. If you use the reminders, your “Said today” and “Fasted today” records are kept there too, and so is everything you make in Study. None of this is sent to us. We do not have a server that receives it, and there is no Bahá’í Reader account, sync service or backup service. The app gives its copy on each device a random identifier, made by the app and not read from your device, so that your notes can be merged correctly if you move or sync them; it is not sent to us.

You can clear your location and your reminder records in Location & reminders, remove highlights, notes and saved passages one by one in the app, and remove Study books from the library and then delete them for good.

Study books

When you import a PDF into Study (from a file dialog or, on Android, with “Open with” or Share), the app copies it into its own folder on your device; the original file is only read. Your highlights, answers, bookmarks and notes for the book are kept on your device with your other notes. The books themselves are never sent anywhere by the app, and on Android they are left out of Android’s backup and out of moving to a new phone.

On Linux, if you also use the Dogear PDF reader, the app looks in Dogear’s folder on your computer for marks on the same book, after you import it or when you choose “Bring in Dogear marks…”. It only reads Dogear’s files and never changes them.

Exporting, syncing and backing up (all optional)

Export. “Export notes…” in Settings writes all your notes, highlights, saved passages, reading positions and Study marks into one file, and the Markdown exports write highlights, notes or a Study book’s answers into a file. Each is saved where you choose, and is yours to keep, move or delete. Your location and the app’s device settings are not in the notes file.

Sync through a folder. If you turn this on in Settings, the app writes the same kind of file, with that random identifier in its name, into a folder you choose, and reads the files your other devices put there. With “Share imported Study PDFs too” (on unless you turn it off), it also copies your Study books into that folder. The app itself still does not go online: keeping the folder the same on your devices is done by a program you choose, such as Syncthing, Dropbox, Nextcloud, OneDrive or iCloud Drive, and that program’s own privacy terms apply to what it copies. Turning sync off stops the app writing there; the files already in the folder are yours to delete.

Android backup. Like most Android apps, Bahá’í Reader lets Android copy its notes and settings (including your location, if you set one) to your Android backup in your Google account and to a new phone. This is done by Android under Google’s terms, not by us, and we cannot see it. Study books are never included. To keep your notes and settings out of it, turn off “Include notes in Android backup” in Settings; this takes effect the next time the app starts.

Location (optional)

The Today page can show sunrise, noon and sunset, and the times for the obligatory prayers and the Fast. To do that it needs to know roughly where you are. You choose how, or not at all:

  • Type a city. The list of cities is part of the app, so searching it does not use the network.
  • Enter a latitude, longitude and time zone by hand.
  • On Android, tap “Use my location”. Only then does the app ask for permission, and only for approximate location (to about a kilometre). It asks the phone once, saves the result on your device, and does not follow your location in the background.

Your location is used only on your device, to work out the sun’s times. It is never sent anywhere. You can clear it at any time.

Notifications and reminders (optional)

On Android and Linux you can turn on reminders, for example for the obligatory prayer or the Fast. They are worked out and shown on your device. On Android the app asks for permission to show notifications and to set alarms at the exact minute, and it sets your reminders again after the phone restarts. Reminders are not available on Windows yet. They are off until you turn them on, and you can turn them off at any time.

The network

English is included in the app, so starting it, reading, searching and using a language you already downloaded never use the network.

On Android, the app does not ask for internet access, so it cannot connect to anything. Languages other than English cannot be downloaded in the Android app for now.

On Linux and Windows, the app connects only when you choose “Download & use”, “Update downloaded content” or “Check available languages”. It then asks bahaiprayers.net, over HTTPS, for that language’s texts or for the list of languages. The request carries only which collection and which language. Like any website, bahaiprayers.net can see your IP address when you connect; their own privacy practices apply to that request. Downloaded texts are saved on your device.

Links in the app or on this site that you choose to open (for example to bahai.org or GitHub) open in your browser, and those sites’ own policies apply.

Children

The app is for anyone who wants to read Bahá’í prayers and writings. It does not collect personal information from anyone, of any age.

This website

This website, bahaireader.org, counts page visits with GoatCounter, which records which page was visited and general details such as the browser, screen size, country and the site you came from. It does not use cookies, and it does not build a profile of you or follow you to other sites. The app itself does not use GoatCounter or anything like it. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, and downloads come from GitHub, so GitHub’s own privacy statement applies to those requests.

Changes and questions

If how the app handles information ever changes, this page will change first, with a new date at the top. The app’s code is open, so you can check any of this on GitHub. If you have a question or a concern, please open an issue.

Bahá’í Reader is an independent community project. It is not an official publication of a Bahá’í institution.
